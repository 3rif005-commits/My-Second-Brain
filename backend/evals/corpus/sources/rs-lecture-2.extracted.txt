## 1.  Data   Collection   Methods   for   GIS  (0:41–3:10)
Core   Concept
Three   distinct   approaches   exist   for   acquiring   geographic   data .  Ground - based
methods   involve   direct   field   or   office   measurement .  Remote   sensing   collects
data   from   a   distance   without   physical   contact   with   the   target   object .
Detailed   Explanation
Data   collection   employs   three   primary   methods :
Field   Survey  ( Miydan ):   Direct   on - ground   measurement   using   instruments
( levels ,  theodolites ,  total   stations ,  GPS )
Office   Survey  ( Maktabi ):   Data   transfer   via   internet ,  digitization   of   paper
maps ,  digital   scanning   of   maps
Remote   Survey  ( Bua ' d ):   Data   acquisition   from   airborne   sensors  ( aircraft ,
drones )  or   spaceborne   platforms  ( satellites )
Lecture 2 RS: Definition, Applications and Data Sources for RS 1
[page 2]
Field   and   office   methods   are   collectively   called   Ground - Based   Methods
( Tareeq   Qaida   al - Ard ) — conducted   from   Earth ' s   surface .  Remote   sensing methods   are   distance - based ;  the   sensor   may   be   on   a   tower ,  crane ,  aircraft ,  or
satellite . Procedures
|Method|Steps|
|---|---|
|Field Survey|(1)Set up instrument (level/theodolite); (2)Measure distances with<br>tape/laser; (3)Record coordinates; (4)Verify measurements|
|Office Survey|(1)Access online sources; (2)Download maps/data; (3)Digitize paper<br>maps via scanner; (4)Process in GIS software|
|Remote Survey|(1)Mount sensor on airborne/spaceborne platform; (2)Image target<br>area; (3)Transmit data; (4)Process imagery|

Core   Concept
A   sensor  ( Mustasheet )   is   a   device   that   detects   and   responds   to   physical
phenomena .  Remote   sensing   is   the   science   of   obtaining   information   about
Earth ' s   surface   without   physical   contact   by   detecting   reflected   or   emitted electromagnetic   energy .
Definitions
|Term|Definition|
|---|---|
|Sensor|A device that can detect and respond to physical stimuli (motion,<br>light, heat).Example: motion sensor at bank doors, lighting sensor<br>in classrooms.|
|Physical Effect|The trigger stimulus: movement, light, heat, or other<br>electromagnetic phenomenon.|
|Contact-Free<br>Principle|Information is gathered from a distance; no direct physical contact<br>between sensor and target object.|
|Remote Sensing|The acquisition of data about Earth's surface without direct contact<br>by recording and processing electromagnetic energy reflected or<br>emitted from objects.|

Human   vision   exemplifies   remote   sensing :  through   eyes ,  you   observe   distant
objects   and   obtain   information   without   touching   them .  Sight   is   the   only   human
sense   that   functions   as   a   true   remote   sensing   system — it   captures   data   from
objects   at   a   distance   with   no   physical   contact .

Core   Concept
Remote   sensing   depends   on   two   energy   sources : (1)  reflected   solar   energy
and  (2)  thermal   energy   emitted   by   all   objects   above  0  Kelvin .  The   sensor
records   and   processes   these   energies   to   extract   environmental   data .
Energy   Types  &  Mechanisms
|Energy Type|Source|Description|
|---|---|---|
|Reflected<br>(Reflected)|Sun|Electromagnetic waves from sun strike Earth;<br>portion reflects back to sensor. Sensor records<br>reflected energy (Passive Remote Sensing).|
|Emitted<br>(Thermal)|Target Object|Any object above 0K (−273°C) emits thermal<br>energy. Sensor records emitted energy (Active or<br>Passive depending on context).|
Any   object   with   temperature  >  0  Kelvin  (−273° C )   emits   electromagnetic
energy .  This   thermal   radiation   is   continuously   detected   and   recorded   by   IR
sensors   on   satellites   and   aircraft .
Key   Variables
|Symbol|Meaning|Units|
|---|---|---|
|_T_|Temperature of object|Kelvin (K) or °C|
|_λ_|Wavelength of electromagnetic<br>radiation|Micrometers (μm) or nanometers<br>(nm)|
|_E_|Reflected or emitted energy|Joules (J) or Watts (W)|

## 4.  Sensor   Classifications :  Passive   vs .  Active  (13:19– 18:01)
Core   Concept
Two   sensor   types   exist   based   on   energy   source .  Passive   sensors   rely   on
external   energy  ( sun ,  thermal ).  Active   sensors   emit   their   own   energy   and
detect   the   return   signal .
Classification   Table
|Type|Energy Source|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Passive|Sun; natural thermal<br>emission|Optical satellites, thermal infrared cameras|
|Active|Sensor itself<br>(transmitter)|Radar (measures vehicle speed by emitting<br>waves and detecting return)|
1.  Radar   emits   electromagnetic   waves   toward   a   vehicle
2.  Waves   strike   the   vehicle   and   reflect   back
3.  Radar   receives   the   return   signal
4.  Time   delay   and   Doppler   shift   measure   vehicle   velocity
Procedures   for   Each   Sensor   Type
|Passive|Active|
|---|---|
|(1)Solar radiation strikes Earth; (2)<br>Object reflects portion; (3)Sensor<br>captures reflected energy; (4)Signal<br>converted to data|(1)Sensor emits EM waves; (2)Waves strike<br>target; (3)Energy reflects back; (4)Sensor<br>detects return; (5)Time-of-flight calculates<br>distance|

Wavelength   Range :   Operates   in   the   non - visible   spectrum ,  sensitive   up   to

## Worked   Examples
Example  1:  Oil   Spill   Detection
Given :   Water   baseline   reflectance   in   IR   is   known
Step  1:   Pristine   water   reflects   at   known   levels   in   IR   bands
Step  2:   Oil - covered   water   shows   anomalous   reflectance  ( typically   lower )
Step  3:   Compare   baseline   vs .  observed   reflectance
Step  4:   Identify   contaminated   areas
Conclusion :   Change   in   spectral   signature   indicates   pollution
Example  2:  Vegetation   Health   Assessment
Given :   Healthy   vegetation   has   NIR   reflectance  > 0.4;  diseased   has   NIR
reflectance  < 0.2
Step  1:   Acquire   Color   IR   image   of   field
Step  2:   Measure   pixel   brightness   in   red   channel  (=  NIR   reflectance )
Step  3:   Bright   red  =  healthy ;  dark   red  =  stressed
Step  4:   Generate   health   map
Result :   Pinpoint   diseased   plants   for   targeted   treatment
Example  3:  Soil   Moisture   Classification
Given :   Wet   soil   absorbs   more   water ;  dry   soil   reflects   more   light
Step  1:   Acquire   multispectral   imagery
Step  2:   Compute   vegetation   index  ( NDVI )  from   NIR   and   red   bands
Step  3:   Correlate   index   with   soil   moisture   field   measurements
Step  4:   Classify   pixels   into   wet / dry   categories
Result :   Irrigation   scheduling   map
Example  4:  Camouflage   Detection  ( Military   Application )
Given :   Green   paint   reflects   low   NIR ;  natural   vegetation   reflects   high   NIR

Step  1:   Acquire   Color   IR   imagery   over   suspected   military   positions
Step  2:   Observe   false - color   map

Step  4:   Blue   features   among   red  =  camouflaged   target

## Summary   of   Key   Takeaways
1.  Three   data   collection   methods   exist :   Ground - based  ( field / office )  and   remote
( airborne / spaceborne )
2.  Two   energy   sources :   Reflected   solar   and   emitted   thermal
3.  Two   sensor   types :   Passive  ( rely   on   external   energy )  and   active  ( emit   their
own )
4.  Five   film   types :   Each   reveals   different   spectral   information ;  color   IR   uses   false
colors   to   highlight   vegetation   and   camouflaged   objects
5.  Spectral   signatures   are   unique :   Different   materials   absorb / reflect / emit   EM
radiation   differently — this   is   the   basis   of   remote   sensing   interpretation
6.  False - color   imaging   is   powerful :   Natural   color   cannot   distinguish   artificial
grass   from   natural   grass ;  Color   IR   reveals   the   difference   instantly
7.  Applications   are   broad :   From   agriculture   to   defense   to   environmental
monitoring
