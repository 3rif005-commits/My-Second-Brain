What is Bias in ML?

 ■   Algorithmic bias: systematic, unfair discrimination in model
     outputs that creates different outcomes for different groups,
     where group membership should not matter.

 ■   Key nuance (important):

 ■   Not all differences are bias
                                                                                   3
 ■   Bias = difference + unjustified + harmful

The 4 Types of Bias — Overview

 ■     Where bias enters the pipeline

       Historical       Representation     Measurement         Aggregation

     past society      data collected     labels defined     model design

                                                                              7

Historical Bias

 ■     Historical bias : the world is unfair, data remembers

 ■     Definition : Past social inequalities reflected in training data.

 ■     Exemple :
 ■     Loan model trained on past approvals learns that certain zip
       codes (historically redlined) have higher default rates, even if
       current residents are equally creditworthy.                                       4

                                                                             8
[page 5]
                                                                        05 /1 2 /2 0 2 6

Representation Bias

 ■   Representation bias : missing voices = blind spots

 ■   Definition : Training data does not reflect the real population.

 ■   Example
 ■   Medical image model trained mostly on fair-skin photos →
     performs poorly on darker skin tones.

                                                                           9

Measurement Bias

 ■   Measurement bias : wrong proxy, wrong conclusion

 ■   Definition: Features or labels are systematically different across
     groups.

 ■   Example
 ■   Using healthcare costs as proxy for "health need." Lower access
     to care  lower recorded costs  model thinks group needs less                   5
     care.

                                                                          10
[page 6]
                                                                     05 /1 2 /2 0 2 6

Aggregation Bias

 ■   Aggregation bias : one-size-fits-all fails

 ■   Definition: Forcing same model requirements on groups with
     different base rates.

 ■   Example
 ■   Single recidivism model for two neighborhoods with different
     crime rates and policing intensity. Looks "accurate" on average,
     wrong for both.

Why Should YOU Care?

 ■    Three reasons (even if you just want good models)

 1.   Business risk: lawsuits, audits, brand damage

 2.   Model quality: bias = poor generalization to new groups

 3.   Ethics: you have power. Power creates responsibility.

Case Study #1 — Amazon Hiring

 Back in 2014:

 ■   Amazon was growing very fast

 ■   Hiring thousands of people every year

 ■   Getting millions of resumes each year

 ■   Screening by hand was slow and not the same for everyone

                                                                  17

The goal — automate resume screening

 Then engineers thought:

 ■   If algorithms can recommend products...

 ■   Why can't they recommend resumes?

 ■   Could we automate resume screening?

The goal — automate resume screening

 The idea:
 ■  Use machine learning to screen resumes
 ■  Same way Amazon recommends products
 ■  Train the algorithm on past resumes and hiring decisions
 ■  Let it learn which resumes led to hires
 ■  Then let it rank new resumes automatically
 ■  Faster. Scalable. Always consistent.

                                                                 19

The goal — automate resume screening

 ■   So they built a tool to screen resumes automatically

 ■   But soon they ran into a problem:
      •   The algorithm learned from old resumes
      •   And old resumes came from people already hired
      •   Most of those people were men

    So the algorithm learned to prefer men                                10

The goal — automate resume screening

 ■   The model learned bias, not merit !

 ■   It started penalizing resumes with the word "women"
 ■   Downgraded graduates from all-women's colleges
 ■   Assigned lower scores to resumes with explicitly female-
     sounding activities

 ■   Why ?
 ■   Past data: tech industry was male-dominated  model learned:
     "male" = "better hire"

Type of Bias – Historical Bias

 ■   Follow patterns from history
 ■   Apply them to new resumes
 ■   Hidden patterns included unfair ones
 ■   Amazon engineers did NOT program the AI to discriminate
 ■   The AI learned it by itself
 ■   Pattern it saw: Most successful past hires = men
 ■   So resumes like those  higher score
 ■   To the machine, it was just math. Not intention.

    This is called: Algorithmic bias or Statistical discrimination

                                                                       23

What Amazon Did

 Timeline:
 ■  2014: Amazon starts building the tool
 ■  2015: Internal team discovers gender bias
 ■  2017: Amazon discontinues the tool

 ■   Engineers tried to fix it:
      •   Removed gender-related words
      •   Adjusted the model                                                      12
      •   Ran new tests
      •   The bias kept coming back (through hidden correlations)

 ■   Features or labels are systematically different across groups.

Case Study #4 — COMPAS

Deeper Problem

 ■   Base rate = actual re-offending rate in the population

 ■   White defendants ~35%
 ■   Black defendants ~52%

     impossible to have equal false positive rates AND equal false
