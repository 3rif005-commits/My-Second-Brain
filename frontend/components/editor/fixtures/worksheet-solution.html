Exercise 1 · Car: acceleration, force and work

Given: m = 1200\,\text{kg} · u = 10\,\text{m/s} · v = 25\,\text{m/s} · t = 6.0\,\text{s} Find: a, F, W

a) Acceleration

Formula: uniform acceleration

a = \frac{v - u}{t}

Steps: a = \frac{25 - 10}{6.0} → 2.5\,\text{m/s}^2

Result: a = 2.5\,\text{m/s}^2

b) Work done by the resultant force

Formula: work–energy theorem

W = \Delta KE = \tfrac12 m (v^2 - u^2)
Steps

  1. Initial kinetic energy → \tfrac12 \times 1200 \times 10^2 = 60\,\text{kJ}

  2. Final kinetic energy → \tfrac12 \times 1200 \times 25^2 = 375\,\text{kJ}

  3. Take the difference:

    W = 375 - 60 = 315\,\text{kJ}

Check: by a second route, s = \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2a} = 105\,\text{m} and W = F s = 3000 \times 105 = 315\,\text{kJ}.

Result: W = 315\,\text{kJ}

Takeaway: 315 kJ is 5.25 times the starting 60 kJ while the speed only rose 2.5 times, because kinetic energy grows with v^2.

Exercise 2 · Unit of momentum

Which is the SI unit of momentum?
A) N · B) kg·m/s · C) J/s · D) kg·m/s²

Formula: definition of momentum

p = m v
Steps
  • A ✗ the newton is a force
  • B ✓ kg × m/s
  • C ✗ J/s is the watt, a power
  • D ✗ the newton again, written out

Result: B, kg·m/s

Trap: A and D are the same unit. When two options are equal, both are wrong.

Exercise 3 · Terminal speed with a parachute

Explain why a skydiver falls at a lower terminal speed once the parachute opens.

Formula: terminal speed is reached when the forces balance

F_{\text{drag}}(v) = m g

Result: The canopy raises the drag at every speed, so drag balances the weight at a lower speed.

  • When the canopy opens, drag exceeds the weight, so the net force points up and the skydiver slows down.
  • Slowing lowers the drag, until it equals the weight again, at the new, lower terminal speed.
